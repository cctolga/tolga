A fast, light player first.

Tolga Player is built on mpv and FFmpeg, the same engines as IINA. Its window and controls come from SDL and Dear ImGui, the libraries games use, and are drawn by the graphics card, so everything stays smooth. It opens at once, plays 4K with hardware decoding and is a 20 MB download. The Apple TV part is one click away.

Starts in about a second

No waiting while a whole movie is converted. The TV starts right where you paused on your computer, and jumping ahead takes a moment.

Questions

Which Apple TV do I need?

An Apple TV with tvOS 26 or later, on the same network as your computer. Tolga Player finds it on its own.

Which computers does it run on?

Macs with Apple Silicon (M1 and later) and macOS 12 or later, and 64-bit PCs with Linux: Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12, Fedora 36 or newer, and most other distributions since 2022. Versions for Intel Macs and Windows are coming soon.

How do I install it on Linux?

There is nothing to install: the Linux version is a single file (AppImage). Download it, make it executable (right-click > Properties, or chmod +x) and open it. AirPlay and the license work the same as on a Mac, and one license key covers both. Download for Linux

Does it lower the picture quality?

No. The picture is sent exactly as it is in the file, 4K and all. Only sound the Apple TV can't play, like DTS or TrueHD, is converted, to Dolby Digital Plus with the 5.1 channels kept.

Can't I just mirror my screen?

Only on a Mac: Linux and Windows PCs can't mirror to an Apple TV at all. And even on a Mac, mirroring sends a recording of your screen: the picture is compressed again, surround sound becomes stereo, and your computer has to keep the movie on screen the whole time. Tolga Player sends the movie file itself, so you get the original picture and 5.1 sound, and you can keep using your computer.

What about VLC or IINA?

Both are great players, and Tolga Player runs on mpv, the same engine as IINA. But neither VLC nor IINA can send a movie to your Apple TV. On a Mac you can mirror your whole screen; on Linux or Windows, not even that. Tolga Player sends the movie itself, with every audio track and subtitle.

Are my movies uploaded anywhere?

No. Tolga Player sends them straight from your computer to your Apple TV over your home network. There is no account and no cloud.

What happens after the 14 days?

Tolga Player keeps playing everything on your computer for free, forever. Sending to the Apple TV asks for a license key, which you get by email when you buy.

Is Tolga Player open source?

The player is, under the Apache License 2.0; its source code is on GitHub. AirPlay is a separate, closed-source part of the official app, and that is what a license unlocks.
